Congratulations, and welcome to Mentorship Circles

Whether you are here to lead or to grow, you are joining a small circle of women in tech who will know your goals, cheer your wins and help you think through the hard moments over the next six months.

“Ambition gets you off the ground. Mentorship takes you to new altitudes.” - Lara Gureje, WIT+ SFBA President

Your expedition starts here

To help every circle reach new heights together, we designed the journey for our inaugural Mentorship Circles cohort as an expedition. Your Circle Lead is your guide. Your circle is the expedition team. Over six months you climb together, one camp a month, toward the summit: session 6, our celebration.

At the summit, every member shows real, observable progress on 1 or 2 personal goals, and the team celebrates the goals it reached together.

Choose your role and your trail map appears. Tap any flag to see what happens at that camp, what to watch out for, the tools you need and a quick checklist. Lace up: the summit is waiting.

This example shows Circle 1: Stepping up: leadership, pay and promotion, led by Jatinder. Every circle follows the same route and the same check-ins; only the topics follow each circle's theme.

What this read covers

How every session runs

  • Prep, 30 minutes, a week ahead: the prep email, the content, 2 or 3 questions.
  • Meet, 60 minutes, the same agenda every time: a round with an altitude check, 25 minutes of content, 20 of discussion, next steps, close.
  • Log, 2 minutes, right after.

The whole journey

  • Session 1, Base Camp: kickoff, the Circle Agreement, summit goals and a first topic.
  • Sessions 2 to 5, Camps 1 to 4: one topic each.
  • After session 2: the membership checkpoint.
  • Three member check-ins, inside sessions 1, 3 and 5, and two optional lead huddles (late January, late March).
  • Session 6, the summit: your readout and the celebration, by the end of June 2027.

Your commitments as Circle Lead

  • Your time: about 1 to 2 hours a month, for six months.
  • Run six sessions, about one a month, with the shared agenda.
  • Make sure everyone who attends signs the Circle Agreement.
  • Must End sessions 1, 3 and 5 with the 3 to 5 minute member check-in, and make sure every member answers it.
  • Log each session in 2 minutes.
  • Send a one-page readout within a week of session 6.
  • Pass membership, money and complaints to the WITSF Program Lead.

What good looks like by session 6

  • Clear progress on 1 or 2 goals for every member, goals each member chose for themselves.
  • A clearer picture of your support: if you use the optional Mentor Map, you can see who helps you grow, then and now.
  • A shared pack of resources and lessons: the talks, tools, books and contacts members brought and discussed.
  • A 3-minute story from every member: what I learned, what I do next, and one thing I could now teach someone.

The route and summit briefing (7 minutes)

  1. Show the route: six sessions, about one a month, finishing by the end of June 2027. The same agenda every time.
  2. Describe the summit: progress on 1 or 2 goals for everyone, a clearer picture of each member's support (the optional Mentor Map), a shared pack of resources, and a 3-minute story each at session 6.
  3. Explain what we track, and why: your 2-minute log after each session, three short member check-ins, and who sees what. It helps us improve the next round, and it never grades anyone.
  4. Set the tone: progress, not pressure. Members choose their goals and what they share. What is shared in the circle stays in the circle. What you learn can leave with you.
  5. Start with the end in mind: show the readout questions now, so everyone knows what you will celebrate together.

Goals into a plan: write every goal where all can see it, group the ones that are alike, compare them with your circle's topic path, and adjust until most members see their goals. Write the plan into the Circle Agreement and keep to it.

Three questions to ask: What support would you like from me, and from each other? Do you like to be encouraged, challenged, or both? If this circle made a real difference by June, what would that look like?

Pick a format

  • Guest speaker: 25 minutes, then discussion. Ask the WITSF Program Lead if you need a name.
  • Watch together: a 10 to 18 minute talk. Each topic card suggests one.
  • Your story: a moment from your career that taught you something about the topic.
  • Member-led: a member who knows the topic leads the first half. Great for confidence.
  • Hot seat: one member brings a real challenge. For 20 minutes the circle asks, using GROW: Goal (what do you want?), Reality (what is happening now?), Options (what could you do?), Will (what will you do, and by when?). Ask first, advise last.

Your role: do and avoidWhat you do, what you don't, what we give you.›
  • You do: set the dates, run each session with the shared agenda, line up its content, make room for every voice, and log the session in 2 minutes.
  • You don't: need every answer, fix anyone's career, or handle membership, money or complaints. Those come to the WITSF Program Lead.
  • We give you: this kit, the introduction to your circle, help finding a guest speaker, an anonymous summary of member feedback, and two optional huddles with the other leads.
DoAvoid
Be a sounding boardFixing the problem for them
Ask what they will tryTelling people what to do
Challenge, warmlyPushing too far, too soon
Listen with curiosityJudging, or jumping to advice
Follow through on what you offerPromising what you cannot deliver

Great mentors see the whole person, not only the career. Life outside work is welcome in the room, at the pace each member chooses.

Circle 1 topicsOur topic path, and why these topics.›

Suggested path for Circle 1: Stepping up: leadership, pay and promotion. Members' goals at session 1 confirm or change it.

Why these topics: when they signed up, members of this circle most often asked for help with pay and promotion, leadership and visibility, and most also want clarity on their next career step. This path tackles those requests together, one topic a month, and at session 1 the circle can swap a topic, for example career direction, to fit its goals.
Session 1 Leadership: managing people and stepping into senior roles
Why it matters
The step from doing the work to leading people is one of the biggest in a career, and few people are taught how.
Questions
  • What was, or will be, your hardest shift from doing to leading?
  • How do you give feedback that lands and keeps trust?
  • What does senior look like in your company, and who shows it well?
Format idea
The Circle Lead tells the story of a leadership mistake and what it taught, then open discussion.
Take-home action
Ask one person for feedback on how you lead or influence.
Resource
Roselinde Torres: What it takes to be a great leader (TED)
LeeAnn Renninger: The secret to giving great feedback (TED)
Session 2 Visibility, personal brand and executive presence
Why it matters
Good work does not always speak for itself. People who decide on your next step need to know what you do and how you think.
Questions
  • Which three words do you want people to use about you?
  • Who decides on your next opportunity, and what do they know about your work?
  • Where could you speak up more this month?
Format idea
Each member gives a 60-second pitch. The circle names one strength they heard.
Take-home action
Share one recent win with your manager, or in a short LinkedIn post.
Resource
Amy Cuddy: Your body language may shape who you are (TED)
Julian Treasure: How to speak so that people want to listen (TED)
Session 3 Negotiating pay, scope or promotion
Why it matters
What you do not ask for, you rarely get. Preparation makes the conversation easier on both sides.
Questions
  • What do you know about your market value, and where can you learn more?
  • What is your ask, and what is your walk-away point?
  • What makes negotiating hard for you?
Format idea
Practice in pairs: make the ask, hear a pushback, respond. Swap roles.
Take-home action
Write your promotion or pay case on one page.
Resource
Adam Galinsky: How to speak up for yourself (TED)
Session 4 Building a network and finding sponsors (including navigating politics)
Why it matters
Mentors advise you. Sponsors spend their influence on you. A strong network has both, and it grows by giving first.
Questions
  • Using the optional Mentor Map: which seats are filled today, and which are empty?
  • Who could be a sponsor for you, and do they know your work?
  • What can you offer someone before you ask for anything?
Format idea
A Mentor Map exercise in pairs, using the optional Mentor Map, then each member names one person to reach out to.
Take-home action
Book one coffee chat with someone outside your team (the member kit has a short how-to).
Resource
Carla Harris: How to find the person who can help you get ahead at work (TED)
Tanya Menon: The secret to great opportunities? The person you haven't met yet (TED)
Adam Grant: Are you a giver or a taker? (TED)
Karen Wickre: Taking the Work Out of Networking, an introvert's guide (book, Gallery Books, 2018)
Session 5 Confidence, work-life balance and tough moments
Why it matters
Confidence grows from small acts of courage, and a circle is a safe place to practise them.
Questions
  • When did you last feel truly confident at work, and what was different?
  • Which boundary would help you most this month?
  • Who do you lean on in a tough week?
Format idea
The Circle Lead shares a tough moment first, then members share as they wish. Confidentiality matters most here.
Take-home action
Set one boundary for the next month, and tell the circle how it went.
Resource
Reshma Saujani: Teach girls bravery, not perfection (TED)
Brené Brown: The power of vulnerability (TED)

Ground rulesMustMustTrust, channel, attendance, membership.›
  • Trust: What is shared in the circle stays in the circle. What you learn can leave with you. Sessions are not recorded.
  • Channel: Email is the official channel for introductions, agendas, materials and decisions. A group chat is optional and opt-in for every member. Nothing confidential goes in it, and the program team is not in it. Chat tool: TBC.
  • No reshuffling: There is no re-matching or moving between circles.
  • Membership: Anyone who is not a WIT+ SFBA member cannot go beyond session 2. If membership is not in place by session 3, the member steps off the circle warmly, with an open invitation to join a later round. The WITSF Program Lead handles it.

How to ask for a coffee chat

  • A short subject line that names something specific: their talk, their post, or the skill you hope to learn.
  • One genuine reason you are writing to them, and anything you have in common.
  • A small ask: 20 minutes, at a time that suits them.
  • Thank them, in the first note and again afterwards.
Example: "Hi Priya, your talk on moving from engineering into product stayed with me. I am exploring the same move. Could I borrow 20 minutes in the next few weeks to hear what helped you most? Thank you either way."
Mentor Map (your base crew)Optional: who helps you grow, then and now.›

Your Mentor Map is a self-serve guide, not a required form. It is a simple way to picture the people who help you grow: a mentor, a connector, an advisor, a peer, a coach and a sponsor. Fill it in for yourself if it helps: at your first session and again at the end, or only at the end. Its purpose is to help you see whether this circle helped you build connections that matter across those roles, and which seats you still want to fill. No one collects it, and empty seats are normal.
